The HUNITY (NMHH-1) satellite

The satellite Data decoder

The HUNITY (NMHH-1) satellite was launched with the support of the NMHH and the Faculty of Electrical Engineering and Information Technology, Department of Broadband Communications and the Radio Club Association of the Budapest University of Technology and Economics.

The Seraph board flies on HUNITY, a 3PQ PocketQube satellite. One PocketQube unit is a 5 cm cube, and 3PQ means three of them in a row, so the satellite measures 5 × 5 × 15 centimetres.

Sun-synchronous orbit

In a sun-synchronous orbit the satellite passes over a given latitude at roughly the same local time every day. For this, the orbital plane slowly turns, by exactly as much as the Earth moves around the Sun in a year. An inclination of about 97.4° means that the orbit passes close to the poles.

Seraph sits on the satellite's deployable experiment panel. Its light sensor clearly shows when the satellite enters the Earth's shadow and when it comes out again.
